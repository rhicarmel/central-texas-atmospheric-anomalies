# Project Context

## Background

Central Texas experiences severe thunderstorms, tornadoes, flash flooding, hail, and other hazardous weather. These events can develop rapidly and affect communities across a region with substantial geographic and atmospheric variability.

This project was created to investigate whether aerosol observations provide additional information about tornado occurrence beyond established meteorological variables such as atmospheric instability, moisture, and wind shear.

## Public-Safety Motivation

Severe-weather forecasting has direct consequences for public safety. Even modest improvements in identifying hazardous conditions could support earlier warnings, better preparedness, and more informed emergency planning.

This project is motivated by the effects that unexpected and rapidly developing storms have had on communities in Central Texas. Its long-term purpose is to explore whether publicly available environmental data can contribute to a clearer understanding of local severe-weather patterns.

## Scientific Context

Aerosols are small solid particles or liquid droplets suspended in the atmosphere. They can interact with radiation, clouds, precipitation, and atmospheric processes. However, tornado formation involves complex interactions among instability, moisture, wind shear, storm organization, and other meteorological conditions.

The project therefore does not assume that aerosols cause tornadoes. It will test whether aerosol measurements:

1. Are associated with conditions surrounding documented tornado events.
2. Provide predictive information after conventional meteorological variables are included.
3. Improve model performance on unseen time periods.

Any observed relationship will be interpreted cautiously. Predictive value, statistical association, and physical causation are separate claims requiring different forms of evidence.

## Geographic Focus

Central Texas was selected because it is locally meaningful and experiences considerable variability in terrain, land cover, atmospheric conditions, and severe-weather hazards.

The exact study boundary has not yet been finalized. It may be defined using:

- A selected group of Central Texas counties
- A geographic bounding box
- A meteorologically relevant region surrounding the San Antonio–Austin corridor

The final boundary will depend partly on tornado-event counts, monitoring-station coverage, satellite-data availability, and computational feasibility.

## Data-Science Context

The study will integrate environmental observations collected at different spatial and temporal scales, potentially including:

- NOAA tornado and severe-weather records
- NASA satellite aerosol observations
- Meteorological reanalysis data
- EPA ground-level air-quality measurements
- USGS terrain and elevation data

The initial experiment will compare a meteorology-only baseline model with an aerosol-enhanced model. This design will help determine whether aerosol variables add measurable predictive information rather than merely appearing correlated with storm conditions.

## Operational Context

On July 27, 2026, the day this project was formally organized, NOAA announced an expansion of its cloud-computing infrastructure to include operational weather-prediction models, with Google Cloud supporting National Weather Service forecasting systems.

This announcement places the project within a broader movement toward cloud-based, data-intensive, and machine-learning-assisted weather prediction. It is relevant as professional context, but it is not evidence for the project’s aerosol hypothesis.

## Project Principles

The project will follow these principles:

- Preserve a clear distinction between curiosity, hypothesis, experiment, and evidence.
- Treat missing measurements as unavailable data rather than evidence of atmospheric conditions.
- Retain quality-assurance information whenever available.
- Avoid causal conclusions based only on correlations or predictive performance.
- Document important methodological decisions and revisions.
- Use reproducible workflows and openly available data where practical.
- Report findings that contradict the hypothesis as clearly as findings that support it.
